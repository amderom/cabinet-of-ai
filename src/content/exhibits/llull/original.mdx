## The pitch to the Pope

<div class="original-intro">Naples, 1294. Llull’s petition to Celestine V and his cardinals: the opening, his offer, and the close.</div>

Since God created man first of all so that man might remember Him, understand, love and honour Him, and serve Him; and since so many unbelievers are going to everlasting fire, who neither remember Him, nor know Him, nor love Him, and this from the moment this world was created until the time we live in; and since there are so many that, I believe, for every Christian there are a hundred or more who are not Christians: it would be most fitting that you, supreme holy Bishop Celestine the Fifth, who were elected pope by the Holy Spirit, and you, honoured and discreet lord Cardinals, should open the treasury of Holy Church, to bring it about that those who are in error, and neither know nor love God, may come to the light of truth and follow the end for which they were created.

I, Ramon Llull, unworthy, believe I have many such reasons, by a certain new method that God gave me, to overcome all those who would prove or disprove anything against the Catholic faith.

I could give many other reasons, but I fear to say too much; and if I say too much, I beg and ask your pardon. And, putting in order what I ask, I ask leave of you, holy Father, and of you, lord Cardinals: may it please you to send me, unworthy as I am, first to the Saracens, to honour our Lord God among them. This petition was given in the city of Naples to the holy Father Celestine the Fifth and to the honoured lord Cardinals, in the year 1294.

<Latin>

Cum Deus principaliter creaverit hominem, ut homo ipsum recolat, intelligat, amet, honoret, et ipsi serviat, et cum sint tot infideles euntes ad ignem perdurabilem, qui illum non recolunt, nec cognoscunt, nec amant, et hoc quamprimum hic mundus fuit creatus usque ad hoc tempus, in quo sumus: et etiam cum sint tot, quod credo, quod pro uno Christiano sint centum vel plures qui non sunt Christiani, multum esset conveniens, quod vos supreme sancte Episcope Coelestine Quinte, qui per Sanctum Spiritum estis electi in papam, et Domini honorati et discreti Cardinales aperiretis thesaurum S. Ecclesiae ad procurandum, quomodo illi, qui sunt in errore et Deum non cognoscunt nec amant, venirent ad lumen veritatis, et sequerentur finem, propter quem sunt creati.

Ego Raymundus Lullus indignus, aestimo me multas tales [rationes] habere secundum aliquem novum modum, quem Deus mihi dedit ad vincendum omnes illos, qui contra Fidem Catholicam aliquid volunt probare vel improbare.

Multas alias rationes possem dicere, sed timeo nimium loqui, et si nimium loquor, supplico et peto veniam, ut mihi remittatur; et ponendo ista, quae peto, in ordine, peto veniam a vobis sancte Pater et a vobis Dominis Cardinalibus, ut vobis placeat me indignum primum mittere ad Saracenos ad honorandum inter illos nostrum Dominum Deum. Data est haec petitio in civitate Neapolitana sancto Patri Coelestino Quinto, et honoratis Dominis Cardinalibus Anno MCCXCIV.

</Latin>

## How to teach the Art

<div class="original-intro">Pisa, 1308. The last chapter of the <i>Ars brevis</i>, Llull’s short version of his Art, and its closing lines.</div>

This part is divided into four.

First, the artist must know the alphabet well, by heart, and the figures, the definitions, the rules and the layout of the table.

Second, he must explain the text to the students by reason, and not tie himself to other people’s authorities. And the students must read the text through, and if they are in doubt about anything, ask the artist, that is, the master.

Third, the master or artist must set questions in front of the students and solve them by reason, following the Art’s procedure. For without reason the artist cannot use this Art well. Know, then, that this Art has three friends: subtlety of intellect, reason, and good intention. Without these three, no one can learn it.

Fourth, the artist must set the students questions for them to answer, and tell them to multiply reasons towards one and the same conclusion, and to find the places that let them answer and multiply reasons. And if the students cannot answer, nor multiply reasons, nor find the places, then the master must teach them all of the above.

To the honour and praise of God, and for the public good, Raymond finished this book at Pisa, in the monastery of Saint Dominic, in the month of January, in the year 1307 of the Incarnation of our Lord Jesus Christ (January 1308 by our reckoning), to whom be praise and honour for ever and ever. Amen.

<Latin>

Pars ista dividitur in quatuor partes.

Prima est ut artista bene sciat alphabetum cordetenus, & figuras, diffinitiones, & regulas atque assituationem tabulæ.

Secunda pars est ut ipse declaret benè textum scolaribus rationabiliter, & non liget se cum auctoritatibus aliorum. Et quod scolares perlegant textum, & si dubitaverint in aliquo petant ab artista sive à magistro illud.

Tertia pars est quod magister sive artista faciat quæstiones coram scolaribus & solvat eas rationabiliter secundum artis processum. Nam absque ratione artista non potest bene uti ista arte. Vnde sciendum est quod hæc ars tres amicos habet, videlicet subtilitatem intellectus, & rationem, & bonam intentionem: sine istis quidem tribus hanc artem nullus addiscere potest.

Quarta pars est quod artista faciat scolaribus quæstiones ut ipsi de illis respondeant: & dicat illis quod multiplicent rationes ad unam & eandem conclusionem, & adhuc inveniant loca quorum ratione sciant respondere & rationes multiplicare.

Si verò scolares nesciant respondere, nec rationes multiplicare, nec loca invenire, tunc temporis magister doceat scolares de prædictis.

Ad honorem & laudem Dei & publicæ utilitatis finivit Raymundus hunc librum, Pysis in monasterio Sancti Dominici in mense Ianuarii, in anno Domini M.CCCVII. incarnationis Domini nostri Iesu Christi, Cui sit laus & honos per infinita secula seculorum Amen.

</Latin>
