# How Llull's Art works — the author's notes, 2026-11-13

> Sent by the author as the concrete basis for the machine. It need not look like this: the demo must stay "a fun, light experience", so some things will be sacrificed (and perhaps explained in the essay). Facts marked by her as needing a check are to be checked against Bonner, *The Art and Logic of Ramon Llull*, or the Ramon Llull Database.

## Core idea

- Every concept is a letter: B C D E F G H I K (A = God, the origin).
- The same 9 letters are reused across different sets, so one combination can be read in many contexts.
- You rotate or enumerate combinations, then read each one as a statement or a question.

## The data (all 9-item lists, B→K)

- Divine dignities (Fig. A): Goodness, Greatness, Duration, Power, Wisdom, Will, Virtue, Truth, Glory.
- Relational principles (Fig. T): three triads: Difference / Concordance / Contrariety; Beginning / Middle / End; Majority / Equality / Minority.
- Questions: Whether? What? Of what? Why? How much? What kind? When? Where? How/with what?
- Subjects: God, Angel, Heaven, Man, Imagination, Sense, Vegetative, Elemental, Instrumental.
- Virtues: Justice, Prudence, Fortitude, Temperance, Faith, Hope, Charity, Patience, Compassion.
- Vices: Gluttony, Avarice, Lust, Pride, Sloth, Envy, Wrath, Lying, Inconstancy.

## The figures (the machine parts)

1. Fig. A: 9 points on a circle, with all lines between them (K9, 36 pairs). Each pair is a proposition. "BC" reads both "Goodness is great" and "Greatness is good". Pairs are convertible, so treat them as unordered, or generate both readings.
2. Fig. T: the same idea with triangles, linking relational principles. It is used to test relations such as concordance and contrariety.
3. Third figure: a half-matrix table of all pairs (BC, BD … IK).
4. Fourth figure: three concentric rotating wheels, each carrying B–K. Rotating them aligns triples, and the "Table" lists them as 84 columns (9 choose 3) of chambers. Sources differ on chamber details (the total is usually given as 1,680).

## The procedure

1. Pick a question (one of the 9).
2. Pick a subject (e.g. Man, God).
3. Spin the wheels or enumerate combos to get letter groups.
4. Substitute each letter's meaning from the relevant list.
5. Test the resulting statement against the rules and the principles' definitions. Anything that contradicts God's dignities, or a principle's definition, is rejected.

## Checked by the agent, 2026-11-13 (sources below)

**Dates.** *Ars generalis ultima* written 1305–1308 (begun in Lyon, finished in Pisa); *Ars brevis* 1308 (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Ramon Llull": "the *Ars generalis ultima*, written between 1305 and 1308, and the *Ars brevis*, from 1308").

**Eternity or Duration: both are Llull's.** D is *duratio* in the ternary Art (SEP gives "duration", and the definition "Duration is that through which goodness, greatness etc. are lasting"); the 1517 Lyon edition prints **DURATIO** on its *Prima figura* (scan n33) but **ETERNITAS** in its own alphabet table a few pages earlier (scan n6, *Principia transcendentia*: Bonitas, Magnitudo, Eternitas, Potestas, Sapientia, Voluntas, Virtus, Veritas, Gloria). Bonner's English gives "eternity or duration"; in the earlier quaternary Art the dignity is called Eternity. So neither word is wrong: pick one for the site and use it everywhere.

**The alphabet: six meanings per letter** (SEP table): dignities; relations (difference, concordance, contrariety, beginning, middle, end, majority, equality, minority); questions (whether?, what?, of what?, why?, how much?, what quality?, when?, where?, how and with what?); subjects; virtues; vices.

**How the figures are actually used:**
- *First figure* (A): the nine dignities in a circle, each joined to every other; any two are mutually predicable ("goodness is great", "greatness is good").
- *Second figure* (T): the nine relations as three triangles, each relation split into species (e.g. difference between sensual and sensual, sensual and intellectual, intellectual and intellectual).
- *Third figure*: the 36 pairs (BC … IK). "Evacuating" a pair draws out every sense: BC gives 12 statements (goodness is great, difference is great…), then subject and predicate swapped, then turned into questions: 36 per pair.
- *Fourth figure*: three concentric circles; the outer is fixed, the two inner ones turn. Each alignment gives a triple (BCD, BCE … HIK): **84 triples, the 84 columns of the Table.**
- *The Table*: each column has **20 chambers** (84 × 20 = 1,680), e.g. column BCD runs bcd, bcdt, bctb … tbcd. **T is a switch**: letters before T take their first-figure meaning (dignities), letters after T their second-figure meaning (relations). bctb = goodness, greatness + difference.
- *Answering a question* (narpan.net, Part 5, worked example "Is the world eternal?"): find the question's terms (eternity → D; "whether" → B), go to the column that holds them (BCD), take the chambers in order, draw one reason from each by applying the definitions and the rules, and reject whatever contradicts them. The first chamber's reason: an eternal world would produce only good, yet evil exists; Llull concludes "the world is not eternal."

Sources: [SEP, Ramon Llull](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/llull/); [Lullian Arts, Ars Magna Part 5, The Table](https://lullianarts.narpan.net/Ars-Magna/5.htm) (translator not named on the page); 1517 Lyon edition, Internet Archive [illuminatisacrep00llul](https://archive.org/details/illuminatisacrep00llul), scans n6 and n33.
