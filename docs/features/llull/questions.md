# Llull's machine — the nine questions (Task 1, for Checkpoint A)

> Validated by the author on 2026-11-13 (questions, verdicts, sources); **reasons rewritten the same day to follow Llull's own route** (her request: "make them faithful"), to be validated again. This file is the source of truth: the code's `questions.ts` is tied to it by a test.
>
> **Sources of the English.** Ramon Llull, *Ars generalis ultima* (1305–1308), trans. Yanis Dambergs © 2003, [lullianarts.narpan.net](https://lullianarts.narpan.net/Ars-Magna/contents2.html), quoted briefly and credited (the author's decision, 2026-11-13). Every quotation in «guillemets» is checked word for word against his pages by script (see the end). His spelling is kept inside quotations ("honored").
>
> **How each question plays.** The visitor taps a question. Its three **letters** light up on the wheel (the **column**, one of Llull's 84). Its **reasons** appear one by one, each naming the letters it combines. Then the **verdict**, with its **source line**.
>
> **Llull's two routes, both his own.**
> - **Through the Table** (Part 5): Llull runs "Is the world eternal?" through column BCD, chamber by chamber. In a chamber, the letters before T are read as qualities (Goodness, Greatness…) and the letters after T as relations (Difference, Concordance…): BCTB is "Goodness + Greatness, through Difference". **R1 follows this route.**
> - **Through the subjects and the hundred forms** (Parts 9 and 10): for the questions of Part 11, Llull points to a numbered paragraph where the question's subject is combined with one principle or rule ("go to #19"). **R2 to R6 follow this route**: their letters are the subject and the principle or rule Llull names there, and their reasons are his own sentences from that paragraph.
> - **The three start-up questions** are ours, answered only with Llull's definitions (Part 3) and his own analogies.
>
> **Format of a reason.** `**letters** — what they combine`, then **Plain:** the reason in one simple sentence (what the page shows first, the author's request for newcomers; shown in prototypes v4–v6, she said "go on" on 2026-11-13), then the reason with Llull's own words, then where it comes from in Llull. The page shows the plain sentence, then Llull's quoted words under it.

## Real questions (6)

### R1 · Is the world eternal?

- **Letters:** B (whether) · C (greatness, implicit) · D (eternity)
- **Column:** BCD
- **Reasons:**
  1. **BCD** — Goodness + Greatness + Eternity
     - Plain: If the world were eternal, it would be eternally good, and there could be no evil in it.
     - An eternal world would make good last for ever, so there could be no evil in it. «But there is evil in the world as we know by experience.»
     - Llull: chamber BCDT (Part 5, ch. 1)
  2. **BC** — Goodness + Greatness, through Difference
     - Plain: Then there would be two eternities, God's and the world's. That makes no sense.
     - Two eternities, God's and the world's? Difference makes that a confusion.
     - Llull: chamber BCTB (Part 5, ch. 2)
  3. **BC** — Goodness + Greatness, through Concordance
     - Plain: An eternal world would be in eternal harmony. Yet it is full of conflict.
     - An eternal world would hold eternal concordance, but contrariety opposes it.
     - Llull: chamber BCTC (Part 5, ch. 3)
- **Verdict:** **No.** «Therefore we conclude that the world is not eternal.»
- **Source line:** *Ars generalis ultima*, Part 5, "One single question", chambers 1–3 of 20.

### R2 · Is the imagination of irrational animals less than that of humans?

- **Letters:** B (whether) · F (the imagination) · K (minority)
- **Column:** BFK
- **Reasons:**
  1. **FK** — The imagination + Minority
     - Plain: The lesser a thing, the closer it is to nothing. Animal imagination is drawn to small things.
     - «By reason of minority, the imagination is close to naught and it tends toward this minority by objectifying minor objects.»
     - Llull: Part 9, the imagination combined with the principles, #19
  2. **BF** — Whether + the imagination
     - Plain: Animals can't grasp what things really are. If they could, they would be doing science.
     - Do animals reach the essence of things with their imagination? «The answer is no, or else, they would be making science, which is impossible.»
     - Llull: Part 11, questions on the imagination
- **Verdict:** **Yes.** «For instance, no irrational animal can imagine a mountain made of gold.»
- **Source line:** *Ars generalis ultima*, Part 11 (questions on the imagination) and Part 9, #19.

### R3 · Why do people enjoy new things?

- **Letters:** E (why; beginning) · H (when: time) · I (where: place)
- **Column:** EHI
- **Reasons:**
  1. **HI** — Time + Place
     - Plain: Nothing can be new without time and place.
     - «Newness cannot exist without place, time and motion.» Llull adds that rules H and I, when and where, agree.
     - Llull: Part 10, the hundred forms, Newness, #81
  2. **E** — Beginning
     - Plain: Everything new began somewhere. There was a first new thing.
     - «There had to be a first newness just as there was a first motion, time and place.»
     - Llull: Part 10, the hundred forms, Newness, #81
- **Verdict:** «Because we are on a road.»
- **Source line:** *Ars generalis ultima*, Part 11 (questions on newness) and Part 10, #81.

### R4 · Is it legitimate to lie in order to communicate a greater truth?

- **Letters:** B (whether) · I (truth) · K (minority)
- **Column:** BIK
- **Reasons:**
  1. **IK** — Truth + Minority
     - Plain: A small truth is enough to expose a lie.
     - «Falsehood is clearly revealed by minor truth.»
     - Llull: Part 9, falsehood combined with the principles, #245
  2. **BI** — Whether + Truth
     - Plain: A lie never acts like the truth. That is how the truth finds it out.
     - «Falsehood does not act with acts of truth, hence it follows that it is detected with acts of truth.»
     - Llull: Part 9, falsehood combined with the principles, #244
- **Verdict:** **Yes**, «on the condition that falsehood is not a cause but an occasion.»
- **Source line:** *Ars generalis ultima*, Part 11 (questions on falsehood) and Part 9, #244–245.

### R5 · Why can someone who strives to be honored never find his fill of honor?

- **Letters:** C (what: rule C) · E (why) · H (majority)
- **Column:** CEH
- **Reasons:**
  1. **C** — What honour is
     - Plain: Honour is something you give, and something you receive.
     - «Honor is an active habit of the one who gives honor and a passive habit of the one who is honored.»
     - Llull: Part 10, the hundred forms, Honor, #115
  2. **H** — Majority
     - Plain: Whoever gives honour stands higher than whoever gets it.
     - «The one who honors is greater than the one honored.»
     - Llull: Part 10, the hundred forms, Honor, #115
- **Verdict:** «Because only God must be honored by men.»
- **Source line:** *Ars generalis ultima*, Part 11 (questions on honour) and Part 10, #115.

### R6 · Why is a glutton never satisfied?

- **Letters:** C (gluttony) · E (why; beginning) · G (end)
- **Column:** CEG
- **Reasons:**
  1. **CG** — Gluttony + End
     - Plain: Gluttony never brings rest.
     - «The gluttonous man finds no repose in gluttony.»
     - Llull: Part 9, gluttony combined with the principles, #173
  2. **CE** — Gluttony + Beginning
     - Plain: It starts with joy and ends in sadness.
     - «Gluttony brings joy in the beginning but sadness in the end.»
     - Llull: Part 9, gluttony combined with the principles, #173
- **Verdict:** «Because he is seeking satisfaction through gluttony.»
- **Source line:** *Ars generalis ultima*, Part 11 (questions on gluttony) and Part 9, #173.

## Start-up questions (3)

Labelled on the page: **"Asked in 2026. Answered with Llull's definitions."** The reasons quote only Llull; the verdicts are ours, in his manner, validated by the author.

### S1 · Will AI take my job?

- **Letters:** B (whether) · G (will) · K (the instruments)
- **Column:** BGK
- **Reasons:**
  1. **GK** — Will + the instruments
     - Plain: The will is what makes us want things. A tool wants nothing.
     - «Will is that on account of which goodness, greatness etc. are desirable.» An instrument desires nothing.
     - Llull: Part 3, the definitions
  2. **K** — The instruments
     - Plain: A tool only goes bad in bad hands.
     - Llull on instruments: «an instrument can be evil when used by an evil man for an evil end.»
     - Llull: Part 11, questions on prudence
- **Verdict:** **No.** An instrument has no will. Ask who holds it.
- **Source line:** Asked in 2026. Answered with Llull's definitions (*Ars generalis ultima*, Parts 3 and 11).

### S2 · Should we pivot?

- **Letters:** E (beginning) · F (middle) · G (end)
- **Column:** EFG
- **Reasons:**
  1. **EG** — Beginning + End
     - Plain: A beginning only rests when it reaches its end.
     - «The end is that in which the beginning rests.»
     - Llull: Part 3, the definitions
  2. **F** — Middle
     - Plain: The middle is the road from the beginning to the end.
     - «The middle is the subject through which the end influences the beginning.»
     - Llull: Part 3, the definitions
- **Verdict:** **No.** A beginning rests only in its end. Turn away from it, and you begin again with nowhere to rest.
- **Source line:** Asked in 2026. Answered with Llull's definitions (*Ars generalis ultima*, Part 3).

### S3 · Will the Pope fund my Art?

- **Letters:** B (whether) · E (power) · G (will)
- **Column:** BEG
- **Reasons:**
  1. **E** — Power
     - Plain: Power is being able to act. The Pope can.
     - «Power is that on account of which goodness, greatness etc. can exist and act.» The Pope can.
     - Llull: Part 3, the definitions
  2. **G** — Will
     - Plain: Will is wanting to act. Does he?
     - «Will is that on account of which goodness, greatness etc. are desirable.» Will he?
     - Llull: Part 3, the definitions
- **Verdict:** **Yes, on paper.** It took five of them and twenty-seven years. *(The author's wording, 2026-11-13.)*
- **Source line:** Asked in 2026. Answered with Llull's definitions (*Ars generalis ultima*, Part 3) and his life (E. Allison Peers, *Ramon Lull: A Biography*, 1929).

History behind S3 (on plate II): Llull pitched five popes from 1285 to 1305: Honorius IV, Nicholas IV, Celestine V, Boniface VIII and Clement V. In 1312, under Clement V, the Council of Vienne ordered Arabic, Hebrew and Chaldaean to be taught in five universities (Peers, p. 360), on paper at least.

## Notes

- **How the cross-references were followed.** In Part 11, each question is followed by its target: "go to #19", or a verse number ("v.6") pointing to the matching sentence of a paragraph of Part 9, where Llull writes one sentence per principle in order (Goodness … Glory, then Difference … Minority). E.g. "Why is a glutton never satisfied? … v.6" → #173, the sixth relation, End: "The gluttonous man finds no repose in gluttony." R4's question carries "v.9" → the ninth relation, Minority, in the falsehood paragraphs.
- **Paragraph numbers in Dambergs' text.** The falsehood paragraph numbered 245 has lost its number on his page (the text runs from #244 to #246); the sentences cited as #245 are those on the relations, after the nine on the dignities.
- **The Lagado essay** now says Llull cranked the same dream "between 1305 and 1308" (her yes).
