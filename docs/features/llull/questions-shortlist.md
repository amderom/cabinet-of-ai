# Llull's machine — question shortlist (draft for the author)

> Drafted 2026-11-13. The machine answers **9 questions**: **6 real ones**, asked and answered by Llull, and **3 start-up ones**, asked in 2026 and answered with Llull's own definitions and analogies, labelled as such on the page. The author picks; nothing goes on the site unpicked.
>
> **Source of the English:** Ramon Llull, *Ars generalis ultima* (1305–1308), trans. Yanis Dambergs, © 2003, [lullianarts.narpan.net](https://lullianarts.narpan.net/Ars-Magna/contents2.html). His notice allows "downloading, printing, and distributing, without modification" for not-for-profit purposes: that permits copies of whole articles, **not necessarily excerpts placed on another site**. So either the author writes to him for permission (her message, her call), or the site's English is **our own translation from the Latin** (1517 Lyon *Ars generalis ultima*, public domain; see `source-texts.md`), with Dambergs used only as a guide. Until then, the quotes below are working text.
>
> **Letters.** Every question gets its letters, as Llull does: the question word (B whether · C what · D of what · E why · F how much · G what kind · H when · I where · K how/with what), the subject (B God · C angel · D heaven · E man · F imagination · G sense · H plants · I elements · K instruments) and the principles involved. These are the agent's mapping, to be checked; Llull himself names the rule or principle in most solutions.

## Real questions (pick 6)

| # | Question | Llull's answer (Dambergs) | Where | Why it's fun |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Is the world eternal?** | Twenty reasons, one per chamber of column BCD; the first: an eternal world would produce only good, yet evil exists. "Therefore we conclude that the world is not eternal." | Part 5, "One single question", ch. 1–20 | **The only question Llull runs through the whole machine himself** (column BCD, 20 chambers). The flagship: strongly recommended |
| R2 | **Can man be sensed?** | "The answer is no, because only accidents can be sensed, but substance cannot be sensed in any way, it can only be understood." | Part 11, sec. 5, ch. 7 (man) | Short, surprising, very Llull |
| R3 | **Is the imagination of animals less than ours?** | "The answer is yes. For instance, no irrational animal can imagine a mountain made of gold." | Part 11, sec. 5, ch. 9 (imagination) | The golden mountain: a perfect shareable line |
| R4 | **Do angels have instruments to hold conversations?** | "The answer is yes, as they have goodness and other principles connatural to them, each of which has its correlatives." | Part 11, sec. 5, ch. 3 (angels) | Angelic chat: an easy wink at messaging apps |
| R5 | **Why do people enjoy new things?** | "Answer: because we are on a road." | Part 11, sec. 6, art. 38 (newness) | Five words; reads like a poem |
| R6 | **Is it legitimate to lie in order to communicate a greater truth?** | "The answer is yes, on the condition that falsehood is not a cause but an occasion." | Part 11, sec. 5, ch. 49 (falsehood) | Real, and it lands on today's "hallucinations" without a word changed |
| R7 | **Why can someone who strives to be honoured never find his fill of honour?** | "Answer: because only God must be honored by men." | Part 11, sec. 6, art. 71 (honour) | LinkedIn, diagnosed in 1308 |
| R8 | **Why is a glutton never satisfied?** | "Answer: because he is seeking satisfaction through gluttony." | Part 11, sec. 5, ch. 37 (gluttony) | Circular and wise at once |
| R9 | **Are some merchants better than others?** | "The answer is yes, because some know better than others how to use the soul's higher and lower faculties." | Part 11, sec. 6, art. 95 (commerce) | Llull on business |
| R10 | **What is hope's enemy?** | "Answer: it is forgetfulness." | Part 11, sec. 5, ch. 27 (hope) | Lovely, short |
| R11 | **Without the motion of heaven, could anything below move?** | "The answer is no…" (fire cannot move water without heaven, "for heaven is prior and fire is posterior") | Part 11, sec. 5, ch. 5 (heaven) | Medieval cosmology in one line; longer answer |
| R12 | **Does the sense perception of a stone remain when the eyes are closed?** | "Answer: it does not remain any longer than its afterimage when the eyes are closed." | Part 11, sec. 5 (senses) | Strangely modern |

**Author's choice (2026-11-13):** R1, R3, R5, R6, R7, R8 (R2 "Can man be sensed?" dropped; the glutton chosen in its place).

## Start-up questions (pick 3)

Asked in 2026, answered only with Llull's own definitions (Part 3) or his own analogies. Each verdict is the agent's draft, for the author to rewrite.

| # | Question | Letters | What the Art reasons with | Draft verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Will AI take my job?** | B whether · K instruments · E man · G will | Llull: "Will is that on account of which goodness, greatness etc. are desirable"; and his own analogy, "an instrument can be evil when used by an evil man for an evil end" | **No.** An instrument has no will. Ask instead who holds it. |
| S2 | **Does the AI understand what it says?** | B whether · F imagination · K instruments · Wisdom | Llull: animals do not attain essences, "or else, they would be making science, which is impossible"; "Wisdom is a property with which the wise understand" | **No**, or else it would be making science. Fluency is not wisdom. (Echoes the essay's Descartes ending) |
| S3 | **Is my startup a unicorn?** | B whether · C greatness · I truth · K glory | "Truth is that which is true about goodness, greatness etc."; "Glory is the delight in which goodness, greatness etc. rest" | **Not yet.** Glory rests only on true greatness. A valuation is not a truth. |
| S4 | **Can growth be infinite?** | F how much · C greatness · D eternity · minority | "Greatness is the being on account of which goodness, eternity etc. are great"; "Minority is being close to nothingness" | **No.** Only God's greatness is without end. Yours is close to minority. |
| S5 | **Should we pivot?** | B whether · beginning · middle · end (second figure) | "The end is that in which the beginning rests" | **No.** A beginning rests only in its end. A pivot is a beginning that never ends. |
| S6 | **Will the Pope fund my Art?** | B whether · E power · G will · K glory | Llull's own story: years of pitching popes and kings for schools and funding (ties to plate II, "The pitch to the Pope") | **The Art says yes.** History says: Honorius, Nicholas, Celestine and Boniface all passed. (Facts to check against the *Vita coetanea*) |

**Author's choice (2026-11-13):** S1 "Will AI take my job?", S5 "Should we pivot?", S6 "Will the Pope fund my Art?".

## How a question plays (for the prototype)

1. Tap a question.
2. Its letters light up on the rings (e.g. B · E · I).
3. The rings turn to its column.
4. Two or three chambers flash past as reasons (one line each).
5. A verdict lands, with its source line: "Ars generalis ultima, Part 11" or "Asked in 2026 · answered with Llull's definitions".
6. Keep or share the answer.

Only R1 has Llull's own chamber-by-chamber reasons; for the others, the "reasons" are his cited principles and definitions, shown as such.
