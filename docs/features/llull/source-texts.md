# Llull exhibit: public-domain source texts for "The original" page

Research date: 2026-11-13. All URLs below were opened during this research unless marked NOT OPENED.
Copyright rule used: US = published before 1931 is PD in 2026 (95-year term); life+70 (UK/EU) = translator/author died before 1 Jan 1956.

---

## 1. Public-domain ENGLISH translation of the Art itself (Ars brevis / AGU / Ars compendiosa / Ars demonstrativa)

**Result: none found.** I found no pre-1931 English translation of any of the Arts, and no 17th-century English printing of one.

What was checked:
- The Online Books Page, Llull author page (https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Llull%2c%20Ramon%2c%201232%3f%2d1316): its English Llull titles are Caxton's *Ordre of Chyualry*, Gilbert Hay's *Order of Knyghthood*, Robert Turner's 1657 alchemical *Of the transmutation of metals*, the *Aurifontina Chymica* (1680), and Peers's translations of the 1920s (*Lover and Beloved* 1923, *Art of Contemplation* 1925, *Blanquerna* 1926, *Tree of Love* 1926, *Book of the Beasts* 1927). None of these is an Ars.
  - *The Art of Contemplation* (1925) is a chapter of *Blanquerna*. It is not the combinatory Art, despite the title.
- Searches of EEBO, ESTC and the web for a 17th-century English Lullist translation turned up only pseudo-Lullian alchemy (1655, 1657), not the Art.
- Internet Archive search for Llull titles: the English items are biographies and Peers's translations only.
- E. Allison Peers, *Ramon Lull: A Biography* (1929) does not translate any passage of the Art.
  - It gives an English paraphrase of the *Ars compendiosa inveniendi veritatem* (Ars magna): figure A as a point in a circle with 16 compartments B–R, and the 120 combinations. See pp. 110–111.
  - The paraphrase is PD (see §2 for status), but it is Peers's description, not Llull's words.
- Samuel M. Zwemer, *Raymund Lull, first missionary to the Moslems* (1902).
  - Opened: https://archive.org/details/raymundlullfirst00zwem (full text read via /stream/…_djvu.txt).
  - Pp. 124–126 describe the alphabet B–K and the fourth figure ("three concentric circles each divided into nine sections … constructed of pasteboard"). The alphabet sentence is a quotation from Edwin Wallace's Encyclopaedia Britannica article (9th ed.).
  - Status: PD in the US (1902). Zwemer died 1952, so PD in the UK/EU since 2023.
  - It is a description, not Llull's text.

Modern translations (copyrighted):
- **Anthony Bonner**:
  - *Selected Works of Ramon Llull*, Princeton 1985, which contains the Ars brevis.
  - *Doctor Illuminatus*, 1993.
  - *The Art and Logic of Ramon Llull*, Brill 2007.
  - All are in copyright. I did NOT open their copyright pages or look for an open licence; I am assuming none, because none surfaced in searches.
- **Yanis Dambergs**, English Ars brevis on lullianarts.net. Opened: https://lullianarts.narpan.net/ (home), /cont.htm, and the PDF https://lullianarts.narpan.net/ArsBrevis.pdf.
  - The home page states that the translations are copyright 2003 Yanis Dambergs. It allows downloading, printing and distributing articles **without modification, for not-for-profit purposes**.
  - So it is not PD. An excerpt on a portfolio site is arguably both a "modification" and outside the grant, so you would need to ask him for permission.
  - The PDF says he consulted Bonner's translation and the Latin ROL XII critical text.
  - It is still useful for checking the alphabet (see §5).

**Recommendation for point 1:** keep the fallback plan. Translate a short Latin passage yourselves from the 1617 Zetzner scan (§3). Your own translation is yours to publish. Llull's Latin (d. 1316) and a 1617 printing are PD everywhere.

## 2. Public-domain English translation of the Vita coaetanea (Mount Randa, the popes)

### Best candidate: E. Allison Peers, *Ramon Lull: A Biography*
- **Edition:** London, SPCK; New York & Toronto, Macmillan, **1929**. xviii + 454 pp.; preface dated Liverpool, October 1928.
- **Translator:** E. Allison Peers, **died 1952** (21 Dec 1952).
- **Status:**
  - US: PD, because it was published in 1929 and US terms for 1929 works ended 1 Jan 2025.
  - UK/EU (life+70): PD since 1 Jan 2023.
  - Peers had no co-translator. The SPCK typographical-arrangement right (25 years) expired long ago.
- **Scan opened:** https://archive.org/details/ramonlullbiograp0000eall (Internet Archive, 484 images).
  - The availability API reports `is_readable: true` and `is_lendable: false`, so it is open to read, not borrow-only.
  - Its OCR full text is at https://archive.org/stream/ramonlullbiograp0000eall/ramonlullbiograp0000eall_djvu.txt (I read it).
  - A second IA copy, `ramonlullbiograp0000eall_y3o5`, exists but I did NOT open it.
  - A PDF at gospelstudies.org.uk claims "Public Domain", but its catalogue page wrongly gives the year as 1898. I did NOT open the PDF itself.
- **Clean transcription:** none (no Gutenberg or Wikisource text). The IA OCR is usable but noisy: footnote markers turn into digits and "Ars" can come out as "4rs". Transcribe from the page images.

What Peers translates from the Vita (he cites his own 1927 translation as "Life"):
- **Mount Randa illumination, p. 105.** He translates the Catalan *Vida coetània* ch. iii (Life p. 12).
  - It begins "as he was engaged in contemplation, with his eyes turned towards the heavens, there came to him in an instant a certain Divine illumination…"
  - It is about 45 words and good for the page.
  - Footnotes on pp. 105 and 109 give the Catalan and Latin originals. The Latin "librum … contra errores infidelium" is noted, as is the descent to La Real: "E encontinent, devallant de la dita muntanya…"
  - The same note quotes Llull's *Disputatio Eremitae et Raymundi*: "Cogito de quadam Arte Generali, quam Deus mihi ostendit in quodam monte." This is a very good short PD Latin line for the page.
- **The popes:** Peers mostly paraphrases here, with short Vita quotations in Catalan or Latin in the footnotes.
  - Honorius IV, pp. 192–193: the only long English passage is a translation of the annals of Spondanus, not the Vita.
  - Nicholas IV, p. 225.
  - Celestine V and the "Petition of Ramon", pp. 251–254: Peers translates parts of Llull's own Petition, which is good for a "pitch" plate.
  - Boniface VIII, p. 256: he quotes the Vita in English, "but for the honour of our Lord he bore them with all gladness"; footnote: V.C. v says he besought the Pope "many times".
  - Clement V, pp. 320–321: the footnote quotes V.C. vi in Catalan: "De la qual cosa axi lo sant Pare com los cardinals hagueren poca cura ne ansia". This translates as "the Holy Father and the cardinals paid little heed or care to it". It suits the parody, and you would translate it yourselves from the PD Catalan.

### The direct translation, not found online: Peers, *A Life of Ramon Lull, written by an unknown hand about 1311*
- **Edition:** London, Burns, Oates & Washbourne, **1927**. This is the full English Vita.
- **Status:** PD in the US (1927) and in the UK/EU (Peers d. 1952).
- **Scan:** I found none.
  - Open Library has it as "no_ebook" (https://openlibrary.org/works/OL22126055W, seen via its search API).
  - It is not on Internet Archive.
  - HathiTrust's catalogue blocked automated access (HTTP 403), so I could NOT check whether HathiTrust holds it.
- To use it you would need a physical or library copy. Worth a manual HathiTrust check in a browser.

### Modern translations (copyrighted)
- Bonner's translation in *Doctor Illuminatus* (1993) and *Selected Works* (1985).
- A. Cortijo Ocaña's English in the IVITRA 15 edition (John Benjamins, 2017).

## 4. Dates

| Work | Begun | Finished | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ars generalis ultima** | **Lyon, November 1305** | **Pisa, monastery of St Dominic, 1308** | Primary + Peers + SEP + Bonner (details below) |
| **Ars brevis** | — | **Pisa, January 1308** | Llull DB + colophon + Peers (details below) |
| Randa illumination (traditional date) | — | **1274** | SEP |

**Ars generalis ultima:**
- Primary: the 1517 Lyon edition (leaf 32) prints the note "Ars ista magna generalis et ultima incepta fuit a Beato Raymundo Lugduni super Rhodanum, mense Novembris, anno Domini MCCCV. Et finita ab ipso in civitate Pisana in monasterio Sancti Dominici … mense Martii, anno Domini MCCCVIII incarnationis…"
- Peers (1929, p. 334 n. 5) quotes the colophon "at Lyons, on the Rhone, in the month of November 1305".
- SEP, "Ramon Llull" (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/llull/; first published 10 Feb 2017, substantive revision 24 Feb 2025): the AGU was "written between 1305 and 1308", finished in Pisa.
- Bonner, "Ramon Llull in 1308" (Llull DB doc, ub.edu/llulldb/docs/Köln.pdf, seen via search snippet only; the PDF would not parse) says Llull completed it "at the beginning of 1308" in Pisa.

**Ars brevis:**
- Llull DB / MedCat: catalogue **III.77**, "expressa – gener 1308", Pisa (https://www.medcat.sciencia.cat/en/medcat?obra=4223, opened).
- The 1617 colophon reads "Ianuarii … MCCCVII incarnationis", an Annunciation-style year that equals January 1308 in modern reckoning.
- Peers p. 335: "(January 1308)".

**Caution on dating styles:** the printed AGU note says *March* 1308, while the Ars brevis says January 1308 (modern). That would put the abridgment *before* the long Art was formally finished. The difference comes from medieval year-reckoning (the Pisan and Florentine calendars differ). Write "1305–1308" and "Pisa, 1308" on the site; don't claim the month for the AGU. I could not open the Llull DB record for the AGU itself to see its exact month.

The SEP confirms the visits to popes: Rome 1287 (Honorius had died) and Nicholas IV; Rome 1290/91 "no beneficial outcome"; Celestine V 1294; Clement V. Peers adds Honorius IV (1285) and Boniface VIII (1295–96), and an interview with Clement at Lyon (1305) or Montpellier.

## 5. The nine letters of the Ars brevis alphabet

Sources checked:
- The Latin of the Zetzner 1617 Ars brevis, leaf 25–26.
- The SEP table (Priani).
- Dambergs's English.

All three agree with your list in substance:

| | Absolute principle | Relative principle | Question | Subject | Virtue | Vice |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B | bonitas | differentia | utrum | Deus | iustitia | avaritia |
| C | magnitudo | concordantia | quid | angelus | prudentia | gula |
| D | duratio (AGU: "aeternitas sive duratio") | contrarietas | de quo | caelum | fortitudo | luxuria |
| E | potestas | principium | quare | homo | temperantia | superbia |
| F | sapientia | medium | quantum | imaginativa | fides | acidia |
| G | voluntas | finis | quale | sensitiva | spes | invidia |
| H | virtus | maioritas | quando | vegetativa | caritas | ira |
| I | veritas | aequalitas | ubi | elementativa | patientia | mendacium |
| K | gloria | minoritas | quomodo et cum quo | instrumentativa | pietas | inconstantia |

Notes, mostly about wording rather than errors:
- **D:** the Ars brevis reads plain *duratio* (Duration). "Eternity" belongs to the AGU's wording. Use "Duration" for the brevis.
- **Subjects F–K are adjectives** in Llull ("the imaginative", "the sensitive", "the vegetative", "the elementative", "the instrumentative"), not "Imagination, Sense…". Your list is a fair gloss. The SEP has "imagination, senses, vegetation, elements, instruments"; Dambergs has "the instrumentative".
- **E, vice:** *superbia*. SEP and Dambergs both translate it "conceit"; "Pride" is the standard rendering and is fine.
- **F, vice:** *acidia*. Prefer "Accidie (sloth)".
- **K, virtue:** *pietas*. SEP and Dambergs give "compassion"; "Pity" is literal. Your "Pity/Compassion" is correct.
- **K, question:** "How? and With what?", two questions under one letter. Correct as you have it.
- **G, question:** "quale", which is "What kind?" or "Of what quality?". Correct.

**Chambers of the fourth figure / table:**
- The Ars brevis says its table has 7 columns that implicitly contain the **84 columns** set out explicitly in the Ars magna (1617, p. 14).
- 84 = the number of three-letter combinations from nine letters (C(9,3) = 84).
- Each column has **20 chambers** (cameras); the SEP: "20 questions in total for each column", and Part 5 draws one reason from each chamber.
- 84 × 20 = **1,680 chambers**. I did not find the figure 1,680 stated in the Ars brevis text itself; it is a derived total. "84 columns of 20 chambers (1,680)" is safe.
