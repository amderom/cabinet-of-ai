## 1 · The quotes

**Title:** It *paraphrased.* I wanted the exact words.

- **me:** Where does this answer come from?
- **agent:** Llull, *Ars generalis ultima*.
- **me:** Which part? Whose translation?
- **agent:** Part 11. My own reading of it.
- **me:** Then it isn't Llull. Quote him word for word, and credit the translator.
- **agent:** Done. Peers, page 321.
- **me:** Page 322. Check again.

**Point:** A quotation is a small contract: these exact words, by this person, found in this place, in this translation. The agent wrote fluently and attributed generously, which, as Descartes said of Llull's Art, is a way of speaking without judgment of what one does not know. So the rule became a habit: every line given to Llull is his, in Yanis Dambergs' translation, credited, and checked by a script against the source; every page number is opened, not remembered; and when the agent reads Llull its own way, the page says so.

## 2 · The research (rewritten at her request: what she asked the agent to research, and her feedback)

**Title:** Swift I knew. Llull, *I had to ask.*

- **me:** I know Gulliver inside out. Llull, much less. Find me an English translation of the Art.
- **agent:** None we may quote freely. The Latin is free: a printing from 1617.
- **me:** Then find out what happened to his pitch to the popes.
- **agent:** Five popes, twenty-seven years. He asked for three colleges and got five.
- **me:** Did the Pope really say yes?
- **agent:** A council decreed it in 1312. [Here's the page.](https://archive.org/details/ramonlullbiograp0000eall/page/360/mode/1up)
- **me:** Okay, I checked. It's fine. Now find me the pitch itself, in his words.
- **agent:** Naples, 1294, printed in full in 1906. [Here's the scan.](https://archive.org/details/bibliotecabiobib01colluoft/page/373/mode/1up)
- **me:** Checked. It's fine.

**Point:** With Swift, I was the specialist and the agent the assistant. With Llull, I sent the agent to the library with a reader's questions: is there a translation? What did the popes say? Did any of it work? It came back with editions from 1517, 1617 and 1906, a biography from 1929, page numbers, and a translation of the Latin. My part: ask for the page, open it, and check. When an answer sounded too neat ("three colleges, got five"), the page said less, so the verdict became "Yes, on paper." Outsourcing research does not outsource doubt.

## 3 · The second time

**Title:** The second exhibit was *easier.* That was the point.

- **me:** Same plates as Lagado?
- **agent:** Shared between the two. Lagado: zero pixels changed.
- **me:** And the credits, page by page, for both.
- **agent:** Done, and written down for the next exhibit.

**Point:** A second exhibit is where a method proves itself. The rules learnt on Lagado (vintage is content, never look; ask before changing; check everything on a phone) were already written down, so the agent followed them instead of discovering them again: seven prototypes instead of twenty-eight. What stayed hard was not the code but the content, which is to say, what Llull actually said.
