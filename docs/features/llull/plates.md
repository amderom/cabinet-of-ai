# Llull's plates, sticker and banner (Task 2, for Checkpoint A)

> Drafted 2026-11-13 for the author to validate. Same format as Lagado's plates: a short face (kind, numeral, caption) and an opened text. Facts from E. Allison Peers, *Ramon Lull: A Biography* (London, SPCK, 1929; public domain), read on the Internet Archive ([ramonlullbiograp0000eall](https://archive.org/details/ramonlullbiograp0000eall)); page numbers are the book's, checked against the scan's running heads. Llull's own figures from the *Ars generalis ultima* (SEP; Dambergs) and the 1517 Lyon edition.

## Plate I · Specimen · "The Fourth Figure"

- **Picture:** the 1517 *Quarta figura* (Lyon, Getty copy), the paper volvelle, as ink.
- **Face:** Specimen · I · *The Fourth Figure*
- **Opened text (draft):**
  > A paper machine from 1308 that answers questions by turning three wheels of letters. Ramon Llull built it to prove the truths of his faith so plainly that no one could refuse them. Pick a question: its letters line up, the Art reasons through its chambers, and the verdict lands.
  >
  > Ramon Llull, *Ars generalis ultima* (1305–1308). Figure from the Lyon edition of 1517.

## Plate II · Portrait · "The Pitch to the Pope"

- **Picture:** the author's ink drawing of Llull.
- **Face:** Portrait · II · *The Pitch to the Pope*
- **Opened text (draft):** a short line, then the funding rounds as a timeline.
  > For twenty-seven years, Llull pitched the same idea to every pope he could reach: schools where missionaries would learn Arabic and the other languages of the East, and argue with his Art.
  >
  > - **1285 · Honorius IV.** Believes in him and backs the teaching of Arabic in Paris. Dies in 1287. *(Peers, pp. 192–3, 224)*
  > - **1288 · Nicholas IV.** The first Franciscan pope. Busy with a crusade, then the fall of Acre. *(pp. 224–5, 233)*
  > - **1294 · Celestine V.** A hermit. Resigns after five months. *(pp. 250–3)*
  > - **1295 · Boniface VIII.** Llull follows him to Rome. Small hope: there is a war on. *(p. 255)*
  > - **1305 · Clement V.** Llull follows him to Lyon. "The Holy Father and the cardinals alike had little care or concern for it." *(Vita coetanea, vi, quoted by Peers, p. 322, note; our translation)*
  > - **1312 · Under Clement V,** a council orders Arabic and Hebrew to be taught in five universities. On paper, at least. *(Peers, p. 360; New Catholic Encyclopedia, "Vienne, Council of")*
  >
  > Portrait of Llull: ink drawing by the author.
- **Translated line:** "De la qual cosa axi lo sant Pare com los cardinals hagueren poca cura ne ansia" (*Vita coetanea*, vi, as given by Peers, p. 322, note). Literally: "Of which thing, the Holy Father as well as the cardinals had little care or concern." Draft for the plate: "The Holy Father and the cardinals alike had little care or concern for it."
- **On her drawing's banderole:** the reading "Lux mea est dominatio" is still to confirm; the plate doesn't depend on it.

## Checkpoint A, validated (2026-11-13)

The author: "I love all the rest." Sticker: **Aligned since 1308**. Banner: **all twelve lines**. Plate texts as drafted, with "decrees" for the Council of Vienne. Checked again at the author's request: the decree is certain (canon 24; Peers p. 360; New Catholic Encyclopedia); Peers's "Rome" is the decree's "wherever the Roman curia resides", which was Avignon at the time; whether the chairs were really set up is not certain (one source says the Arabic chairs never were). So the plate says "decrees" and "the papal court". Simplified at her request ("no one will understand he asked for three colleges and got five"): verdict "Yes, on paper. It took five of them and twenty-seven years."; plate II's last line and banner line 8 ("Funded on paper, 1312") rewritten to match.
